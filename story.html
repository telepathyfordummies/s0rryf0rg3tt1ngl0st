Our Design

Tammy saw snow for the first time.

In it, she saw the deepest of existential dread.

It took hold of her in a way she hadn’t really grasped previously. It made the whole situation feel much more serious. Gravity recalibrated in the freeze. 

The only thing left to anchor her was the person she refused to admit was hers. But she did, all without even doing anything, just by staying calm despite her own unease, just by having a sleeve for Tammy to slip her hand into. Her fingers balled up in the fabric just a little, silently promising Wendy she was still with her. 

The warmth of her hand was enough, Tammy thought. 

The thought slipped away, morphed into morbid fascination. There were so many ends lying around them, trapped in this God awful snowglobe. 

Tammy never let herself think about the future too much, especially not what laid at the end of all of this. She refused to consider the possibility that she wasn’t as brave as she thought she was. She was just living in the moment. 

She would live in every possible moment imaginable, rip it open and savor every last one until she no longer could. 

That was her way of things.

So is life. 

They’d walked for an hour, maybe, before she felt the stiffness in her fingers. She let go to adjust her hand, stretch a little. 

Usually, she was so thoughtless to touch. She got impossibly close without caution. She adored the way she could feel the heat on Wendy’s skin when her cheeks tinted.

But right now, reaching out meant grabbing her hand and for some reason it didn’t feel like something she should just do without thinking. 

Tammy's hand fell to her side. She pretended the few inches between them didn’t feel like a chasm. She persisted. She watched. 

She watched and watched and watched as they walked, the only sounds to stop the maddening silence being the crunch of shoes against snow. 

Then she saw it.

A flicker of movement shattered the stillness. 

Tammy didn’t think to call out. In hindsight, she wondered if maybe some sort of spell was being placed on her. Or maybe she was just as dumb as some of the others thought she was. 

In hindsight she wondered if calling out would’ve changed anything.

It wouldn’t. 

This moment persists.

If it wasn’t here, it was the mountains. 

If it wasn’t here, it was the garden. 

If it wasn’t here, it corrupted home, it made the walls bleed, it killed us all. 

It had to be here, and it was. 

She followed, thoughtlessly, not even ten minutes after realizing that maybe she shouldn’t be so thoughtless, like to do that wouldn’t be shoving down a piece of her everyone loved so dearly. 

No one noticed, of course. 

She slipped away so quietly, so perfectly. 

Tammy was loud. Vibrant. 

Tammy was also completely silent. 

She knew more than anyone else, and she knew more than she let on. 

Most importantly, then and now, she knew how to disappear so easily, even if that wasn’t her intention. 

She drifted away, turned a corner. 

She knew what she saw and she was right because she saw the long brown hair and that ugly beige longcoat she jokingly snarked about growing up. 

The silhouette that made her stomach spin was still walking away from her, unaware she was following.

Tammy, however, stopped dead in her tracks. “Mom?” she called out. 

She didn’t even sound like her. She was off, completely wrong, voice stiff. 

She hadn’t seen her mother since a year before The Severance. The time just… got away. Then it was gone. 

She didn’t think about that either. 

The figure didn’t stop. 

It kept walking. 

Tammy started moving again, pace picking up immediately, closing distance. “Mom!”

 She wasn’t crazy, she wasn’t crazy—It turned a corner in the middle of two buildings.

Tammy started to run. She wasn’t fucking crazy. 

She turned the corner without looking. “Mom!” 

Run.

Run.

Run.

Tammy stopped dead in her tracks, stomach dropping, heart kicking in her chest. She took a step back on instinct, chills running down her spine. Her mouth opened, but no words came out. 

She came face to face with familiarity, but he was wrong. She could see it in his eyes. 

She swallowed once, gaze darting towards the direction she came from for a fraction of a second before locking back onto him.

Tammy’s breath stuttered. “You’re—” 

The words died in her throat.

He regarded her with this softness that was damn near frightening. She knew he was sentimental, but this was so, so wrong. She knew then. He wasn’t supposed to be here, she wasn’t supposed to be here.

“Your mother won’t remember to mourn you, but I always will.”

He said more, she knew that much, but she didn’t want to hear it. A tap to her forehead was enough to make her pause, mind empty. 

It could’ve been a few seconds. 

It could’ve been a few hours. 

It’s hard to tell with him. 

Eventually, another touch pulled her from her stupor. Eventually, she found her way back.

Wendy pulled her back together without knowing. 

She loved her, she loved her, she loved her. 

So thoughtlessly. 

But she couldn’t unknow it. 

Nor could she unknow that she was not crazy. 

Things were going to change, but she had already known that. She had already been promised she would make the right choice. 

For now, she would enjoy being thoughtless. 

For now, she would hug too tight, she would love too hard, she would speak without thinking. 

Finally, she was on the right track.

 



We wouldn’t remember our encounters with him until they were relevant. That was part of his design. Still, he let her keep this knowing she could keep a secret, knowing she knew nothing would change either way. 

This is something I wish I could simply take from your mind and leave in mine, rather than share with you. I would bear every burden between the two of us for you to find peace. I don’t know why it’s the other way around.
